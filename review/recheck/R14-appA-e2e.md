## 1. The probability space

**Objects.**
- Slots are integer indices j ≥ X₀. Slot j has a random position s_j = inf J_j, with J_j = T⁻¹([j+ϑ_s, j+1+ϑ_s)).
- By Lemma adapted, s_j is determined by decisions and offsets at earlier slots.
- The decision is P_j = max(0, 1 − Q_{j−1} − A_j). It is 𝓕_{s_j−}-measurable, because A_j ∈ 𝓕_{s_j−} by Lemma C2.
- A prime placed at slot j lands at s_j + H(s_j)(U_j − ½), with U_j i.i.d. uniform and independent of the past
  (02-construction:201–208).
- 𝓕_t is generated by the decisions and offsets at slots ≤ t.

**(F2) as stated** (05-queue:120–136). Given 𝓕_{s−}, the position of the prime decided at slot s is uniform on
[s − H(s)/2, s + H(s)/2), so its conditional density is ≤ 1/H(s). Whether a prime is decided at s, and the position s
itself, are 𝓕_{s−}-measurable.

**Good events.** Good(<t) is the intersection of the events with time < t and of the queue constraints with t_j < t. It
is 𝓕_{t−}-measurable and shrinks as t grows (05-queue:451–460).

**What each conditional expectation in the PIL uses.** At a pivot p with slot s, the PIL takes E[· | 𝓕_{s−}] of a
nonnegative integrand. The integrand is a function of q_p and of the following, all of which are 𝓕_{s−}-measurable:
- the label s and the labels of the earlier pivots, which are outer summation indices;
- the realised values of the earlier pivots, the free primes of every block decided before s, and the tails;
- the output of earlier Lebesgue integrals, which is a deterministic function of the unprocessed variables;
- the carried factor Φ_{t−1}(s_t), which reads only primes decided before s_t ≤ s;
- 1(G_{<s}).

The late free primes, decided after p, are never inside a conditional expectation. They were replaced earlier by the
pathwise bound (S3)(a), which holds for every ω. So E[original] ≤ E[bound], and the conditioning is applied to the bound.
The order constraints that make the earlier data measurable at s are dropped only after the conditional expectation at s
has been taken. Each such constraint is dropped at the step of its later label.

**Time.** In every use, t_J = t_z = 2z + H(2z). Every pivot slot is ≤ s_max = 2z + 0.51H(2z) < t_z, so
1(G_{<t_J}) ≤ 1(G_{<s_{p₁}}) ≤ 1(G_{<s_{p₂}}) ≤ ⋯ holds. See COSMETIC item 1.

## 2. The PIL induction (A-rankfull:188–323)

**Starting point.** X_𝔰 is a sum, over the slot labels of the pivots, the realised free data and the tails, of
1(slots decided)·∏_i 1(n_i∈J). Every realised configuration satisfies the caps ∏_j 1(q_j ≥ τ_j), so they may be inserted.

**Inductive invariant**, maintained along the decision order, latest first. After each step:
1. The pending integrand, apart from the carried good-event indicator, is a nonnegative function of the unprocessed
   variables. These are the labels and values of the unprocessed pivots, the realised primes of the unprocessed blocks,
   and the tails.
2. As a function of the labels of the run P_t being processed, it depends only on s_t. It does so through Φ_{t−1}(s_t),
   which is non-increasing in s_t, and through the constraint s_t ≥ u_t.
3. The carried indicator is 1(G_{<s′}), where s′ is the slot of the next pivot to be processed.

**Pivot step.**
- Condition on 𝓕_{s−}. Then E[1(prime at s)·Ψ(q_p)·1(G_{<s}) | 𝓕_{s−}] ≤ 1(G_{<s})·H(s)⁻¹∫_{W_s}Ψ dq_p.
- **p is not the last pivot of its run.** Ψ does not depend on s, and 1(G_{<s}) ≤ 1(G_{<s′}).
- **p is the last pivot of its run.**
  - Every admissible s satisfies u_t ≤ s ≤ s_max.
  - Φ_{t−1}(s) ≤ Φ_{t−1}(u_t), since G_{t−1} is non-increasing and Eu_s is non-decreasing.
  - 1(G_{<s}) ≤ 1(G_{<u_t})·1(G_{<s′}).
  - Together: 1(G_{<s})Ψ ≤ 1(G_{<s′})·χ_t·Ψ|_{s_t=u_t}. This holds pointwise in q, in the block data and in ω, and the
    right side is free of s.
- **Label sum.** By Tonelli, Σ_s H(s)⁻¹∫_{W_s}Ψ = ∫Ψ(q)·Σ_s 1(q∈W_s)/H(s) dq.
- **The θ factor.** The inner sum runs over continuous slots. By (F1) and the slot density
  T′_cont ≤ 1 + 1/(κu) + 0.8/log u, it is ≤ 1 + 0.81/log q + 3/H + (variation of H) ≤ θ. This needs q ≥ X₀⁻, which the
  caps give.

**Step (i) commutes with the integrals.** The factors it changes (Φ_{t−1} and χ_t) are constant in q_{P_t}.

**Block step.**
- After the pivots of P_t are integrated, the integrand depends on the block data F_t only through
  χ_t·∏_{g∈F_t} g^{−a_g}·Φ_{t−1}(u_t) (§3, SVL(ii)). The rest of the integrand is free of F_t, including 1(G_{<s′}),
  since s′ is earlier than F_t.
- By Tonelli, the sum over contents goes inside the n-integral. This is valid because after the change of variables the
  domain J^{R≤t} is the same for every content.
- (S3)(a) bounds the sum pathwise by Φ_t(s_{t+1}). This depends only on the label s_{t+1} and on primes decided before
  s_{t+1}, and it is non-increasing. So the invariant holds for P_{t+1}.
- u_t is consumed by the pathwise bound, so it needs no measurability.

**(S3)(a), re-proved by hand** (A-rankfull:413–439).

- The primes with slots in [a, σ) give (E₁/Eu(a))^K, summing over all exponent vectors including the zero vector. The

- For j ≥ 1: 1 − e^{−mv} ≤ mv ≤ (m/j)(e^{jv} − 1). For j = 0: 1 − (1−x)^m ≤ mv.
- G_{t−1}(E₂) ≤ G_{t−1}(y) on [E₁, E₂].
- The intervals [E₁, E₂] of distinct f are disjoint and lie in [Eu(a), A_top] when χ_t = 1, by (d) at u_t ≤ s_max on
  G_{<u_t}.
- This gives ≤ Eu(a)^{−K}∫_{Eu(a)}^{A_top} K y^{j−1} G_{t−1} = Φ_t(a).

**End step (S3)(c).**

- The empty content gives χ_T·Φ_{T−1}(X₀).
- The tails give Σ_d 1/d = Eu_rat for each of the K_T chain rows.
- The product is G_T(Eu_rat) + Eu_rat^{j_T}·G_{T−1}(Eu_rat). The result is deterministic.

### toy_pil.c and toy_pil2.c: Monte Carlo of the whole chain (PIL + SVL + (S3)), k = 1 and k = 2

**Set-up of toy_pil.c.**
- Slots j = 1..100 at positions 20 + j, with box jitter of width H = 4, so θ = 1.
- Rational primes ≤ 19, so tails are 19-smooth and Eu_rat = 5.847.
- The toy good event is Good(<u) = {Eu_s(u′) ≤ A for all u′ ≤ u}.
- Windows J = [z, z+1] with z uniform in [2000, 4000].
- Every ordered pair of distinct elements in J is classified by its skeleton: greedy pivots, E_P and runs.
- The comparison is with the Theorem R envelope θ^kδ^k|det E_P|⁻¹∏τ_j^{−(s_j−1)}·Fin, where Fin = A for k = 1,
  A² for runs (2), and A(A − Eu_rat) for runs (1,1).
- Four adaptive policies:
  - policy 0: always place;
  - policy 1: coin 1/2;
  - policy 2: depends on the previous offset;
  - policy 3: bursty, adaptive.
- Two thresholds: A = Emax = 38.33 and A = Emax/2. 10⁵ samples per run.

**Limitation of the toys.** The envelope has inherent slack: the prime indicator is bounded by 1, and the sums run over all
cofactors, including infeasible ones. So these toys only detect gross errors. In a fixed-slot toy, deciding every slot is
pointwise maximal, so adaptivity matters only through the good event. The expectations are Monte Carlo with 10⁵ samples,
not exact closed forms. Exact checks were done at the level of the components (t1–t6).
