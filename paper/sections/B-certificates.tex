\section{The computer-assisted parts}\label{app:cert}

The scripts are in the directory \texttt{certificates/} of the repository accompanying this paper. They are written in Python~3;
the interval computations use ball arithmetic from \texttt{python-flint} (Arb). Each subsection states what is checked, the
method, the command, and the expected output. Commands are run from the repository root unless stated otherwise.

% Logged correction 54: the constants certificate is a fifth proof step (independent check R8, F2).
Five of them are steps of the proof: the Beurling--Saias certificate (Section~\ref{app:cert-bs}), the finite computations on
the integers (Section~\ref{app:cert-finite}), the small range of the $X_0$-band bound (Section~\ref{app:cert-x0band}), the
numerical constants of Sections~\ref{sec:noise} and~\ref{sec:baserange} (Section~\ref{app:cert-consts}), and the
Rankin constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:rowweights} (\texttt{rankin\_R.py}, Section~\ref{app:cert-rankin}). The
others are independent numerical cross-checks of statements proved in the text (Theorem~\ref{thm:caplw}, Lemma~\ref{lem:sc},
Theorem~\ref{thm:svl}) or of the bookkeeping of Section~\ref{sec:logic}.

\subsection{The Beurling--Saias certificate (\texttt{bs-cert})}\label{app:cert-bs}
\emph{What it certifies.} The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bs} (and of Lemma~\ref{lem:bs-profile}) reduces the induction step to an
explicit inequality $\Sigma(\ell,u)\ge0$, where $\ell=\log Y$, $u=\ell/\log z$ and $\tau=\log t\ge10^8$. Two parts of this reduction
are computer-assisted.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The region $30\tau\le\ell<10^{18}$, $u\ge2$.} A closed-form lower bound $S(\ell,u)\le\Sigma(\ell,u)$ is built from the
majorants of the proof (it is written out in Step~7(iii) of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bs}) and from bounds $r_{\mathrm{low}}\le r\le r_{\mathrm{up}}$ for the Dickman ratio $r=-\rho'/\rho$. The script
checks $S>0$ on cells covering the whole region, uniformly in $\tau\ge10^8$.
\item \emph{One-variable inequalities:} the positivity $c_0(u):=c(u)-\ell\Phi_{\mathrm{ben}}\ge K'u^2\log(1+u)$ for
$27\le u\le4\cdot10^5$, used for $\ell\ge10^{18}$; the one-step inequality of Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-v} for $1\le u\le10^6$; and that one-step inequality on
$u\in[1,2]$ including both extra terms of the proof: the excess $X(u)$ of the small heights $1\le Y'<72$ (present for
$u<1+\log72/L_z$), and a conservative allowance $2K_Mr(u)(e^{0.141}-1)$. Beyond these ranges they are proved analytically.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Method.}
\begin{itemize}
% Logged correction 54: file names, the set of delta, and decimal constants as exact balls (independent check R8, C5, C6, C9).
\item All arithmetic is ball arithmetic (Arb, through \texttt{python-flint} 0.9.0); the bound $S$ is in \texttt{bs\_cert\_arb.py},
the interval layer in \texttt{ivarb.py}. Every elementary
operation and transcendental function is enclosed. The bounds $r_{\mathrm{low}}$ (from the recursion
$r(v)\ge r(v-1)/(1-e^{-r(v-1)})$, $r(2)=1/(2-2\log2)$) and $r_{\mathrm{up}}$ (from $r(x)\le\log((x+1)/\delta)/(1-\delta)$, minimised
over $\delta\in\{0.05,0.1,0.15,0.2,0.3,0.4,0.5\}$) are computed in balls. Decimal constants (such as $K'=2.2$, $K=3.2$,
$C_0=0.70$ and $0.4$) enter as exact balls. Floating-point numbers enter only as exactly representable cell endpoints and
parameters (the float complement $1-\delta$ in $r_{\mathrm{up}}$ is compensated by a factor $1+2^{-50}$), and as
outward-rounded (\texttt{nextafter}) images of ball endpoints: in the table lookups for $r_{\mathrm{low}}$ and $r_{\mathrm{up}}$, in a
few intermediate upper bounds that are fed back into the ball computation, in the final sign test, and in the printed output. Each
such conversion moves a lower bound down or an upper bound up, so it can only weaken the certified inequality.
\item Each cell is the product of an $\ell$-interval and a $u$-interval (or an interval in $v:=u-\ell^{1/4}$). The bound $S$ is
evaluated on the whole box, so no Lipschitz or interpolation argument is needed.
\item The range $\ell\in[3\cdot10^9,10^{18}]$ is split into 403 bands of ratio $1.05$. In each band, $u$-cells cover
$[2,\max(40,u_0/2)]$ and $v$-cells cover the rest up to the largest junction $\bar U(\ell)=U(\ell;\tau=10^8)$. The endpoints of the
$v$-cells are rounded outward, so the seams between $u$-cells and $v$-cells, and the top edge, are covered exactly for every $\ell$ in
the band. Cells that fail are bisected adaptively.
\item Every cell also checks $r(u-1)>\gamma_1$ and $r(u-1)>\gamma_{\max}$, which is the condition for the integrals of the second
majorant to converge.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Finite computations (\texttt{finite-n})}\label{app:cert-finite}
\emph{What it certifies.} The finite computations on $\N$ quoted in Section~\ref{sec:noise}. Each is a supremum or a sum over a
finite range; the numbers refer to the lines of the output.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:cm}(i): the maximum of $(\psi(x)-\psi(x/2))/x$ over $1\le x<1500$, and the elementary bound for $x\ge1500$.
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:cm}(ii): the range of $S(x)-\log x$ on $1\le x\le10^7$, where $S(x)=\sum_{p^k\le x}\log p/p^k$.
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-v}, small heights: the maximum of $\sum_{Y<n\le2Y}1/n$ over $72\le Y<10^4$ and the value
$\log2+10^{-4}$ for $Y\ge10^4$ (line 3a), and the maximum over $1\le Y<72$ (line 3b).
\item The maximum of $y^{-1/2}\sum_{n\le y}\mu^2(n)n^{-1/2}$ over $1\le y\le10^6$, that is, $F_{1/2}$ in the squarefree average of
Section~\ref{sec:noise-bridge}.
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold-mass}: the maximum over primes $p<400$ of $\prod_{q<p}(1-1/q)^{-1}-1.8\log p$.
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:cm}(iii): $\sum_p\log p/(p(p-1))$, as a partial sum to $10^7$ plus an explicit tail bound.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Method.} Each supremum over a real variable is reduced to finitely many points by the step structure of the function; the
reduction is explained in a comment above the corresponding check.

\subsection{The jump at \texorpdfstring{$X_0$}{X0}, small range (\texttt{x0-band})}\label{app:cert-x0band}
\emph{What it certifies.} The small range of Lemma~\ref{lem:xb}, i.e.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:xb-ranges}(i): the bound $B(n)\le5/12$ for every integer $2\le n\le10^6$, where
\[
\begin{gathered}
  B(n)=\frac1n\Bigl[\tfrac12\mu^2(n)+\tfrac12\sum_{d\mid n,\,1<d<n}\Bigl(2\mu^2(d)+\tfrac12\lambda\frac{X(d)}d\Bigr)\Bigr],\\
  X(d)=\sum_{e\mid d,\,1<e<d}\mu^2(e)\,e\,H_{\mathrm{ord}}(d/e),\qquad\lambda=1.0001.
\end{gathered}
\]
% Logged correction 54: exact check of the maximum, and the tail expression of the proof (independent check R8, C7, F2.5).
The maximum is attained only at $n=6$, where $B(6)=5/12$ exactly; the script checks this exactly, comparing every other
(inflated) $B(n)$ with $5/12$ as a rational and recomputing $B(6)$ in rationals. It also prints the suprema over $n\ge10^a$ for
$a=1,\dots,5$, and the analytic tail bound $(\tfrac12+1.64\sqrt n)/n+\tfrac14\cdot7.4915\cdot2^{0.2}n^{-0.2}$ of the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:xb-ranges}(i) at $n=10^6$, which is $0.137383\le0.14$ (the constants $7.4915$ and $0.14$ are certified in
Section~\ref{app:cert-consts}). For $n>10^6$ the lemma uses that analytic bound, not the computation.

\emph{Method.} Sieves compute, exactly in integers: $\mu^2$; $H_{\mathrm{ord}}(m)$ by $H_{\mathrm{ord}}(1)=1$,
$H_{\mathrm{ord}}(m)=\sum_{d\mid m,\,d<m}H_{\mathrm{ord}}(d)$; $X(d)$; and the divisor sums. The integer arrays are checked against
64-bit overflow (the maximum of $X$ is about $2.9\cdot10^8$). The only floating-point steps are the divisions $X(d)/d$ and sums of at
most $\tau(n)\le240$ nonnegative terms, whose relative rounding error is $<10^{-12}$; the script multiplies every $B(n)$ by
$1+10^{-9}$ before taking maxima, so every printed value is an upper bound. All terms are nonnegative and every integer quantity is
exact, so no interval arithmetic is needed.

\subsection{Numerical constants (\texttt{constants})}\label{app:cert-consts}
\emph{What it certifies.} Constants that the text quotes, in ball arithmetic, without another certificate. Here
$A(s):=1/(2-\zeta(s))-1=\sum_{m\ge2}\Hord(m)m^{-s}$ for $\NN=\N$.
\begin{itemize}
\item The ordered factorisations of Section~\ref{sec:kr}: the root $\rho_0\in(1.7286,1.7287)$ of $\zeta(\rho_0)=2$;
$A_0(0.03)=A(1.97)=2.06733\le2.07$; $A_1(0.03)=-\zeta'(1.97)/(2-\zeta(1.97))^2=9.40888\le9.42$; $\zeta(1.8)=1.88223$, so
$\zeta(1.8)+X_0^{-0.7}\le1.883$; and $1/(2-1.883)\le8.6$.
\item Proposition~\ref{prop:xb-ranges}: $A(1.8-3\cdot10^{-6})=7.49142\le7.4915$; $\frac14\cdot7.4915\cdot2^{0.2}\le2.152$;
$4/\sqrt6\le1.64$; the tail $(\frac12+1.64\sqrt n)/n+2.152n^{-0.2}=0.13742\le0.14$ at $n=10^6$; $A(1.77)=13.0822\le13.1$;
$\zeta(1.97)-1=0.67398\le0.674$; and $-\zeta'(1.97)=1.00004\le1.0001$.
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}(c) and \eqref{eq:mu1sum}: $\operatorname{Ein}(2)=\int_0^2(e^w-1)\,dw/w=3.68387<3.69$;
$I_1=14.9154$, $I_2=214.7714$ and $I_3=6.6962$; $(2.02/\log2)(I_1+I_2+I_3)=688.88\le690$; and $690/\Lambda\le7\cdot10^{-6}$.

\emph{Method.} Ball arithmetic (\texttt{python-flint} 0.9.0) throughout: $\zeta$, and $\zeta'$ from the Taylor series of $\zeta$;
$\operatorname{Ein}(2)=\operatorname{Ei}(2)-\gamma-\log2$; $\rho_0$ from the signs of $\zeta-2$ at $1.7286$ and $1.7287$
($\zeta$ is decreasing). $I_1$ is evaluated from its antiderivative, and $I_3$ from the closed form
$2e^\gamma e^{\operatorname{Ein}(2)}\cdot\frac12e^{1/2}E_1(\log4+\frac12)$. For $I_2$, after the substitution $v=e^t$, the range
$[\log4,60]$ is integrated by Arb's certified integration (\texttt{acb.integral}), and the rest is bounded explicitly by
$e^{-60}p(121)(\log121+1.01)/121$ with $p(y)=y^4+8y^3+48y^2+192y+384$ (a comment in the script gives the derivation). Each

\subsection{The capacity theorem: numerical cross-check and Rankin constants (\texttt{capl-cert})}\label{app:cert-capl}
Theorem~\ref{thm:caplw} is proved analytically. Apart from \texttt{rankin\_R.py} (see below), the scripts in
\texttt{certificates/capl-cert/} re-evaluate, in double-precision
floating point, the precise criteria that Steps~2--5 of that proof bound analytically: the degree cap $D^*$ computed from
$\binom{m+k}{k}$; the exact $j=\max\{j:e(r_0(D+1))^{2j+1}<c\}$; the row-weight bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:rowweights}(b),(d); and
condition~\eqref{eq:twelvetwo} of Lemma~\ref{lem:lemmaP} with the exact absorption threshold $y$. They are not interval-certified, and
nothing in the proof depends on them. They use only the Python standard library and each takes a few seconds.
\begin{itemize}

\paragraph{A proof step in this directory: \texttt{rankin\_R.py}.}\label{app:cert-rankin}
Unlike the scripts above, this one is a step of the proof, of Lemma~\ref{lem:rowweights}(c),(e). In ball arithmetic it checks:
\begin{itemize}
\item $R(\sigma):=\sum_p\bigl(-\log(1-p^{-\sigma})-p^{-\sigma}\bigr)$ satisfies $R(0.69)\le0.7618\le0.77$ and $R(0.75)\le0.6212\le0.63$.
The sum over $p\le10^7$ is computed in balls, and the rest is bounded by
$\sum_{n>N}n^{-2\sigma}/(2(1-n^{-\sigma}))\le N^{1-2\sigma}/(2(2\sigma-1)(1-N^{-\sigma}))$ with $N=10^7$.
% Logged correction 54: one evaluation at x = 11 plus monotonicity replaces a grid (independent check R8, C8).
\item $\operatorname{Ei}(11)-e^{11}/11-e^{11}/121\le134$, and $2.75/x+134x^2e^{-x}<1$ for $x\ge11$. Together these give
$\operatorname{Ei}(x)\le(e^x/x)(1+2/x)$ for $x\ge11$. The second function has derivative $-2.75/x^2+134x(2-x)e^{-x}<0$ for $x>2$,
so it suffices that it is $<1$ at $x=11$.
\item $(3/2)^{3/4}\le1.3555$ and $\operatorname{Ei}(\tfrac14\log2)\ge-1$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Lemma~\ref{lem:sc}}{Lemma SC}: numerical check (\texttt{lemma-sc})}\label{app:cert-sc}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sc} is proved in full in Appendix~\ref{app:rankfull}; these scripts are an independent corroboration, not a step of
the proof. They work in exact rational arithmetic (Python's \texttt{fractions}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{What is tested.} Random rank-full integer exponent matrices $E$ ($r\le8$ rows, up to $r+6$ columns, entries in
$\{0,1,2,3\}$ at random densities; columns that vanish identically are removed, since primes absent from every row must not be
counted) whose greedy latest-first pivots form at least two stages. For every \emph{minimum} cover $C$ of the row supports,
\texttt{sc\_check.py} searches by brute force for nested row sets $R_{\le1}\subset R_{\le2}\subset\cdots$ with
$|R_{\le t}|=|P_{\le t}|$, $E_{R_{\le t},P_{\le t}}$ invertible, and every pivot of $P_{\le t}$ and every late free column of $F_t$ covered
by a row of $C\cap R_{\le t}$. \texttt{sc\_greedy.py} tests the explicit construction of the proof: the lexicographically first
bases of the row matroids $M_t$, with the rows of $C$ first, checked for nesting, invertibility and coverage.
\texttt{sc\_check\_independent.py} is an independent re-implementation over all \emph{minimal} covers; it also shows that the order
matters, since putting the rows of $C$ last fails coverage in $1238$ of $2342$ cases.
\item \emph{Commands and expected output} (each run prints one dictionary and takes seconds to a minute):
\begingroup\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
python3 -I -B certificates/lemma-sc/sc_check.py 1
  {'tested': 10615, 'single': 20820, 'all_ok': 10615, 'some_fail': 0, 'all_fail': 0}
python3 -I -B certificates/lemma-sc/sc_check.py 7 20000 7
  {'tested': 5585, 'single': 10441, 'all_ok': 5585, 'some_fail': 0, 'all_fail': 0}
python3 -I -B certificates/lemma-sc/sc_check.py 11 60000 5
  {'tested': 15933, 'single': 31583, 'all_ok': 15933, 'some_fail': 0, 'all_fail': 0}
python3 -I -B certificates/lemma-sc/sc_greedy.py 5 30000 8
  {'ok': 13433}
python3 -I -B certificates/lemma-sc/sc_check_independent.py
  configs 842 {'ok': 2342, 'fail': 0, 'advfail': 1238, 'nest': 0}
\end{verbatim}
\endgroup
Here \texttt{tested} counts the multi-stage configurations (single-stage ones are skipped and counted as \texttt{single}), and
\texttt{all\_ok} those for which the nested sets exist for every minimum cover; \texttt{ok} counts the (configuration, cover) pairs
for which the explicit construction works, and \texttt{advfail} the coverage failures of the reversed order.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The staged volume lemma: structural check (\texttt{svl-stress})}\label{app:cert-svl}
Theorem~\ref{thm:svl} is proved in Appendix~\ref{app:rankfull}; this script is an independent sanity check of the structural facts
its proof uses, not a step of the proof. In exact rational arithmetic, on random and adversarial rank-full configurations with
