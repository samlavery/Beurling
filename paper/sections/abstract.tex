% Abstract (v3 §0.1, §7.1)
We construct a discrete Beurling generalized number system whose integer counting function satisfies
$N(x)=x+O(x^{\theta}\log^*x)$, where $\theta\in[\tfrac1{20},\tfrac12-10^{-4}]$ is fixed and $\log^*$ is the iterated logarithm, and whose
zeta function $\zeta_{\PP}$ has zeros $\rho_k$ with $\operatorname{Re}\rho_k\to1$. In particular $\zeta_{\PP}$ is zero-free in no
half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>1-\eps$. Equivalently, there is a discrete $[1,\beta]$-system with $\beta\le\theta<\tfrac12$. So the
hypothesis $N(x)=Ax+O(x^{\theta})$ with $\theta<\tfrac12$ does not by itself force a zero-free half-plane.

The system is built by a randomised greedy rule with feedback from the integer count. Generalized primes are placed one
slot at a time, against a target, adapted to the construction, whose Mellin transform carries designed zeros, and a prime is
placed only when the queue of composite elements is empty. The heart of the proof is a positive drift for this queue at every scale. It comes from
a sharp resolvent bound of Beurling--Saias type for the realised system, together with a counting theorem for clusters of
composites in short windows.

Several steps are computer-assisted, with certificates in interval or exact arithmetic. The construction, the proofs and the
certificate code were developed with extensive use of AI agents, and so far they have been reviewed only by AI agents; no
human expert has yet verified the proof (see Appendix~\ref{app:ai}).
